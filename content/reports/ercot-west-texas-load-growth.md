## The Setup

ERCOT's large-load interconnection queue stood at 63 GW at the end of 2024. By end-2025 it was 239 GW. That is a 270% increase in twelve months, with 225 new large-load requests filed in 2025 alone versus 152 cumulative during 2022 to 2024. Data centers account for roughly 73% of the queue. This is not incremental growth. It is a structural repricing of the West Texas power market.

The 2023 Enverus blog by Scott Bruns on ERCOT load growth and data center impact laid out the thesis clearly: data centers have already changed the price dynamics at HB_WEST by absorbing generation that previously exported out of the region. My comparison analysis against ERCOT's own board materials, the Potomac Economics IMM 2024 SOM, and PUCT filings confirms the core argument and sharpens it. The grid cannot absorb this demand on the current infrastructure timeline. Reserve margins go negative in 2028.

## The Queue Is Real, but the Haircuts Matter

The 239 GW headline is attention-grabbing. It is also misleading if taken at face value. ERCOT applies planning haircuts of 49.8% for data center Officer Letter Loads and 55.4% for general large loads. The adjusted planning load embedded in the December 2025 CDR is roughly 55 GW, not 239 GW.

That still matters. 55 GW of adjusted new load on a system where total firm capacity is 104,850 MW in summer 2026 is enormous. The December 2025 CDR base case shows summer reserve margins at 18.3% for 2026, which looks comfortable. By 2028 they turn negative at -1.3% at peak load hour and -6.2% at peak net load hour. By 2030 they hit -12.7% and -19.2% respectively.

![Reserve Margin Outlook](/images/ercot-reserve-margin.png)

SB 6, signed by Governor Abbott on June 20, 2025, is the legislative response. It imposes a 75 MW large-load threshold, mandatory curtailment protocols for loads interconnected after December 31, 2025, $100,000 minimum study fees, site-control requirements, and mandatory disclosure of duplicate interconnection requests. The PUCT is running five implementation dockets under Project 58317 with final rules expected by end-2026. Under the CDR's Scenario 1 (SB 6 curtailment) and Scenario 2 (full Texas Energy Fund generation), margins stay positive throughout the horizon.

The directional view: the base case is closer to reality than the optimistic scenarios. SB 6 curtailment authority helps at the margin, but the 9.7 GW of TEF generation that has not met Planning Guide milestones is aspirational capacity, not firm supply. The grid tightens structurally from 2028 forward.

## West Texas Pricing Has Already Shifted

The Enverus analysis flagged the key dynamic: data centers consuming local generation rather than letting it export have eliminated the ultra-low pricing periods that historically defined West Texas. The 2024 data confirms this at scale.

ERCOT system-wide real-time energy prices averaged roughly $29/MWh in 2024, a 52% drop from 2023. But West Texas diverged from that trend. The West Zone surpassed the South Zone in 2024 as ERCOT's highest intra-zonal congestion zone and was the only zone where intra-zonal congestion costs increased year-over-year. LZ_WEST ran persistently higher than HB_WEST because the load zone reflects the premium paid to import power into far-West Texas during non-solar hours.

Negative pricing still hits the West Zone harder than anywhere else in ERCOT, with the floor pinned near the -$251/MWh System-Wide Offer Floor during extreme solar oversupply. The IMM confirms West Texas had the most frequent negative-price hours in 2024. The pricing profile is now bipolar: extreme negatives during solar peak, rising premiums during non-solar hours as data center load absorbs available thermal and wind generation.

Total real-time congestion costs across ERCOT were $1.9 billion in 2024, down 17% from 2023. But congestion from Generic Transmission Constraints rose 10% year-over-year. CRR auction revenue hit $1.7 billion, exceeding payments to CRR holders for the first time since 2015. That efficiency improvement is a small positive. The structural congestion in the West is not.

## Curtailment Is the Symptom

ERCOT curtailed over 8 TWh of renewable generation in 2024. The West Zone accounted for 5.3 TWh of that, split between 3.1 TWh of wind and 2.2 TWh of solar. ERCOT experienced at least one binding transmission constraint 86% of the time, up from 78% in 2023.

![Renewable Curtailment 2024](/images/ercot-curtailment-2024.png)

This is the direct consequence of building renewable capacity faster than transmission capacity. West Texas has the wind and solar resources. It does not have the wires to move the power. Data center load growth partially solves this by consuming generation locally, which is exactly the argument Enverus makes. The problem is that data center demand does not match solar and wind generation profiles. The mismatch creates both the extreme negatives during oversupply and the rising premiums during undersupply.

## The Permian Forecast Is Already Stale

ERCOT's Permian Basin Reliability Plan, filed July 2024, projects total peak demand of 23.7 GW in 2030 and 26.4 GW in 2038. Oil and gas load accounts for 12.0 GW in 2030 and 14.7 GW in 2038. Non-oil-and-gas load (data centers, crypto, hydrogen, industrial) is held flat at 11.7 GW in both years by assumption.

That assumption is already wrong. The 270% queue growth since January 2025 happened after this forecast was set. Commissioner Glotfelty has publicly noted that ERCOT's 2021 Permian study understated actual demand by roughly 60% and Delaware Basin demand by roughly 100%. The July 2024 forecast uses a 2022 S&P oil and gas demand curve. It is 21 months old and does not reflect the current pipeline of large-load interconnection requests.

## The Infrastructure Response: 765-kV for the First Time

The PUCT approved three 765-kV import paths to the Permian Basin on April 24, 2025, the first extra-high-voltage transmission lines in ERCOT history. Oncor's Longshore Switch to Drill Hole Switch path runs roughly 180 miles with a target in-service of December 2028. AEP Texas and CPS Energy's Howard to Solstice path covers 300 to 370 miles from Fort Stockton to San Antonio area. A third path runs to Bell East and Sand Lake area. Collective target in-service for all three is 2031.

Total approved 765-kV mileage for the Permian area is roughly 1,329 miles plus 1,585 miles of new 345-kV. The PBRP all-in cost is $12.95 to $15.32 billion. The statewide 765-kV Strategic Transmission Expansion Plan runs roughly $33 billion. In December 2025 the ERCOT Board endorsed additional 765-kV STEP elements including eastern and western loops.

The timeline problem is straightforward. The first 765-kV line targets December 2028. Reserve margins go negative that same summer. The infrastructure buildout does not arrive in time to prevent the tightest years.

## The Chevron-Microsoft Project Is the Swing Factor

Chevron, Microsoft, and Engine No. 1 entered an exclusivity agreement on March 31, 2026 for a proposed 2.5 GW gas-fired power plant near Pecos, Texas, scalable to 5 GW, with seven GE Vernova 7HA turbines and an estimated cost of $7 billion. The plant would be behind-the-meter and not ERCOT-connected at the outset.

FID has not been taken. Chevron targeted early 2026. The March 31 announcement was an exclusivity agreement only, not a final investment decision. No commercial terms have been finalized. First turbines were expected late 2026 with initial operations in 2027.

This project is the single most important data point for the West Texas power balance through 2030. If it reaches FID and builds on schedule, 2.5 GW of behind-the-meter gas generation absorbs a meaningful slice of the data center demand without touching the ERCOT grid. If it stalls, that demand shows up on the grid and the reserve margin math gets worse.

Meanwhile in Abilene, the Stargate/Crusoe data center campus has roughly 200 MW operational out of a total announced 2.1 GW buildout (1.2 GW original Stargate campus plus a 900 MW Microsoft-dedicated adjacent campus announced March 2026). Microsoft is a tenant of the adjacent campus, not a Stargate JV partner. The Stargate JV is OpenAI, Oracle, SoftBank, and MGX.

## The View

West Texas is repricing structurally. The combination of 239 GW in the queue, 73% from data centers, negative reserve margins by 2028 in the base case, rising intra-zonal congestion, 5.3 TWh of curtailed generation, and 765-kV lines that do not arrive until 2031 points in one direction. This region will see higher average power prices, wider basis spreads, and more volatile pricing through the rest of the decade.

The market is not pricing the full extent of this tightness yet. SB 6 implementation through 2026 will thin the queue by forcing de-duplication and real capital commitments, but it will not eliminate the structural demand growth. The Chevron-Microsoft FID decision is the near-term catalyst that determines whether 2028 to 2030 looks manageable or critical.

Stay long West Texas basis spreads. The congestion premium is structural, not cyclical.
